# Plan — 2026-08-26

## What this was meant to be

Deadline notifications, item #1 on `plan-2026-08-25.md` and jumped four times.
Brief: *inspired by Duolingo, not generic — different and modern.*

## What it turned into first

The brief was possible because Albus already had a mascot: `AlbusCactus` is
drawn in pure geometry and its **spike density encodes workload**, and
`AlbusCore` already had `WorkloadState { calm, busy, cooked }` documented as
"the signal the mascot's mood is derived from".

Checking that claim before building on it found it was not true — and that most
of the ground this feature would stand on was designed, documented, tested, and
connected to nothing.

## What was built

Twelve notification kinds, planned purely in `AlbusCore/Notifications/` with no
`import UserNotifications`, so quiet hours, the 2/day cap, the 64-slot budget and
DST are answered by `swift test`. Delivery is a namespace-scoped diff with a
digest early-out. A settings screen, a delegate for taps and action buttons, and
the cactus riding along as a `UNNotificationAttachment` that bristles with the
real week — no entitlement, no Team ID.

Decisions, from Felipe: **full Duo chaos** for the voice; **real stakes plus soft
momentum, no breakable streak**; **2/day with quiet hours and back-off**.

Two guardrails: a `seriousMode` toggle, and `NotificationKind.allowsChaos` —
`false` for `.overdue`, proven by a test, so a joke can never land on work a
student has already missed.

## Six bugs that every green test missed

All found by looking at output rather than by asserting on it. None was a crash.

1. **"Bio IA in 80 hours"** on a three-hour warning — every time-relative fact
   was measured from when the plan was built, not when it would arrive.
2. **"0 minutes across 0 steps"** — briefs fired on days with nothing scheduled.
3. **Two notifications 30 minutes apart** — a 09:00 deadline put its T-3h at
   06:00, quiet hours moved it to 07:00, and the hand-in warning was at 07:30.
4. **"1 days left", "1 steps remaining"**.
5. **"Sci is the past out and today owes it 162 minutes"** — a brief three days
   ahead named an assignment already overdue by the time it would arrive.
6. **"1220 minutes of work, one evening. Tight but real."** — twenty hours.

1–4 came from printing a realistic week. 5–6 came from reading the simulator's
own `PendingNotifications.plist` — the queue the app had actually handed iOS.
Each is now pinned by a regression test naming where it came from.

Two more were found by the app-side tests: the plan was **not idempotent**
(persisted recent-lines fed back into copy selection, so every rebuild rewrote
every notification), and the coordinator **trusted the client to filter
namespaces** — the invariant that stops a rebuild deleting a running focus
timer's alert.

And one regression I introduced and caught in the simulator: **three
`navigationDestination(isPresented:)` modifiers in one scope**. SwiftUI keeps one
and routes everything through it, so "Month view" opened notification settings.
One enum-driven destination now.
